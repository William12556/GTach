## 1. Summary

`prompt-64d8d8fc-uniform-sweep-colour.md` was implemented in full. Two files:
`src/gtach/display/models.py` at +59 / −11 and `src/gtach/display/manager.py`
at +54 / −54. No departures from the prompt were required and no clarification
was needed.

Four things changed:

**(a) The sweep is one colour.** The six-segment graduated arc is gone. The
filled sweep from 0 to the current RPM is a single `draw_donut_arc` call in
`palette.bands[active_band]`. This reverses the constraint `prompt-5014040c`
imposed twice and emphatically; the reversal is the human decision recorded at
`issue-64d8d8fc` §2.1, and the comment block that stated the old constraint was
replaced along with the code it described.

**(b) The centre disc carries the band.** `Palette` gains `band_centres` and
`band_centres_lit`, six entries each, authored not scaled. The three scalar
`shift_centre_lit` / `shift_centre_normal` / `shift_centre_down` fields are
removed from the dataclass and both palettes, and appear nowhere in the tree.

**(c) One lookup each per frame.** `_get_band_colour` and `_get_shift_cue` are
now called once each, both immediately after the frame's palette read and
before `surface.fill`. Previously `_get_shift_cue` was called twice, at
`manager.py:1198` and `:1336`.

**(d) The boundary marks are 7 px**, matching the major ticks, because they now
carry the whole of the anticipatory cue.

**The border is untouched.** `_draw_shift_border` and the three
`shift_border_*` values in both palettes are byte-identical, as are
`_get_band_colour`, `Palette.bands` and `_band_hysteresis`.

`pytest tests/` is **11 passed**, unchanged. **46 assertions pass** post-change,
covering every scenario in the prompt's `testing` section.

**Not verified on target.** Everything below is bench evidence. The two values
the prompt itself flags as most likely to come back after observation — the
7 px mark width and the `band_centres` luminance — are single literals and were
left exactly as specified.

## 2. What Was Changed

### 2.1 `models.py` — the centre palette moves to the band

`shift_centre_lit`, `shift_centre_normal` and `shift_centre_down` are removed
from the `Palette` declaration and from both instances. `band_centres` and
`band_centres_lit` are declared beside `bands`, not beside the `shift_border_*`
group, as the prompt directed. `shift_centre_dark` is retained — it is the dark
phase of the flash and is not band-indexed.

The values are the prompt's verbatim. Day `band_centres` runs
`(0,0,89)` twice, `(0,89,0)`, `(89,89,0)`, `(89,45,0)`, `(89,0,0)`; night runs
`(0,0,59)` twice, `(0,49,0)`, `(52,49,0)`, `(61,26,0)`, `(70,0,0)`. Night
`band_centres_lit` is equal to `NIGHT_PALETTE.bands`, kept as its own tuple for
structural symmetry and so the two can diverge later without a signature change.

The comment at `models.py:109-112` enumerating *"the five colours returned by
`_get_shift_cue`"* was updated: what it returns is now the three
`shift_border_*` constants plus `shift_centre_dark` and one entry from each of
the two band-indexed tuples.

### 2.2 `manager.py` — `_get_shift_cue` takes the band

Signature is now
`_get_shift_cue(self, rpm: float, active_band: int) -> Tuple[Tuple[int, int, int], int, bool, Tuple[int, int, int]]`,
with `active_band` documented in the `Args` section. The three branches keep
their conditions, their border colours and their border widths verbatim; only
where the centre is read from changes. The flash-phase computation above the
branches is untouched.

The fallback returns `DAY_PALETTE.band_centres[0]` — index 0, not
`active_band`, because the fallback runs precisely when the band cannot be
trusted.

### 2.3 `manager.py` — one lookup, one arc

Both lookups moved to immediately after the frame's palette read at
`manager.py:1148` and before `surface.fill`:

```python
active_band, band_colour = self._get_band_colour(rpm)
border_colour, _, _, centre_colour = self._get_shift_cue(rpm, active_band)
```

The `_get_shift_cue` call at step 1 and the one at step 12 are both deleted;
`border_colour` and `centre_colour` are in scope at both sites. The
`flash_centre` flag was never read at the step-12 site and is discarded into `_`.

Step 4 — the old comment block, the seven-element `segment_bounds` tuple and
the six-iteration loop — is replaced by a single guarded arc. `segment_bounds`
appears nowhere in `manager.py`. The `bands = self.config.rpm_bands` local was
kept in place, since step 9 still needs it.

### 2.4 `manager.py` — the marks

Line width at `manager.py:1315` from 3 to 7, and the step 9 comment extended
with the prompt's text. The 28 px mark length was left alone.

## 4. An Incidental Fix to the change-5012004e Invariant

Collapsing the two `_get_shift_cue` calls fixes a real, pre-existing defect
that the prompt does not mention.

`change-5012004e` reads the palette once per frame into a local so a night
toggle cannot land mid-frame. But `_get_shift_cue` reads `self._palette`
itself, and it was called **twice** — at step 1 for the border and at step 12
for the centre — with the entire face drawn between them. A toggle landing in
that interval produced a border from one palette and a centre disc from the
other.

**Demonstrated, both ways.** A probe returns `DAY_PALETTE` for the first *k*
reads of `self._palette` and `NIGHT_PALETTE` thereafter, sweeping *k*, and asks
whether the border and the centre can disagree:

```
PRE-CHANGE  (HEAD, in a worktree):
  toggle points that split border from centre:
    k=2  border=(0, 180, 0) day    centre=(0, 95, 0) night
    k=3  border=(0, 180, 0) day    centre=(0, 95, 0) night
POST-CHANGE:
  toggle points that split border from centre: none
```

The window is now closed by construction: one call, one read, both values from
the same palette.

A caution for whoever writes the next test here — recorded at §7.1 — is that a
palette which merely *alternates* on every read does **not** discriminate.

## 7. Findings and Observations

### 7.1 An alternating palette is not a discriminating test

My first version of the §4 probe flipped the palette on **every** read of
`self._palette`. It passed against both HEAD and the change, and I nearly
recorded the invariant as pre-existing on that basis.

It passes pre-change by coincidence of parity. Under alternation the
pre-change read order is face → *cue* → bands → *cue*, so the two
`_get_shift_cue` calls land on reads 2 and 4 — the same parity — and agree.
Sweeping the toggle *point* instead of alternating exposes it at k=2 and k=3.
The lesson generalises to anything testing the `change-5012004e` invariant: the
probe must sweep, not alternate.

### 7.2 Bands 0 and 1 are the same blue, and now it is more visible

The prompt names this as pre-existing and out of scope, which it is — the
graduated arc had it too. But the uniform sweep makes it more consequential:
previously the *boundary* between segments 0 and 1 was invisible while the rest
of the arc still gave positional information. Now the entire sweep is one
colour, so crossing `idle_max` produces no visible change at all except the
7 px mark. Not a defect introduced here, and not touched. Worth naming because
the on-target assessment of whether 7 px is sufficient will be most sensitive
at exactly this boundary.

### 7.3 The night flash improves, and this is not incidental

Restated from §3 because it is easy to miss among the constraint checks: the
green flash being replaced measured **2.59:1** in night against
`shift_centre_dark`, below the 3:1 large-text threshold. The band-indexed
replacement measures 3.39:1 at its worst. The upshift cue in night mode is
better than it was, not merely differently coloured.

### 7.4 The polygon reduction is larger than "six to one" at high RPM only

The prompt's performance target says the sweep falls from "up to six
122-point polygons per frame to one". Precisely: the old loop drew one polygon
per band *entered*, so at idle it drew one and only near redline did it draw
six. The saving is therefore RPM-dependent and largest exactly where frame
budget matters least. The `_get_shift_cue` halving, by contrast, is
unconditional. Neither was measured — no timing claim is made here.

### 7.5 `flash_centre` is now returned and never read anywhere

`_get_shift_cue`'s third return element is discarded at its only call site. It
was already unread at the step-12 site before this change, so this is not a
regression, but the tuple now carries a value with no consumer at all. Out of
scope to remove — the prompt fixes the return type — but a candidate for a
future tidy.

## 8. What Was Deliberately Not Done

- **`_get_band_colour` — not one character.** It is absent from the diff.
- **`_draw_shift_border` and the three `shift_border_*` values — untouched.**
  The border encodes what the driver should do; the band encodes what the
  engine is doing. The symmetry argument for band-colouring the border is
  wrong for the reasons the prompt gives, and was not acted on.
- **The flash condition, period and duty cycle — unchanged.** Still
  `rpm >= caution_start`, still derived from `self._frame_counter`.
- **`Palette.bands` and `_band_hysteresis` — unchanged.**
- **The centre colours are not derived by scaling `Palette.bands`.** Authored
  constants, on the reasoning `NIGHT_PALETTE` already records.
- **The 28 px mark length — unchanged.** Only the width moved.
- **Bands 0 and 1 sharing a blue — not "fixed".** Explicitly out of scope
  (§7.2).
- **The `band_centres_lit` entries 0–2 are defined but never displayed.** Kept
  for indexing uniformity, and flash contrast is not asserted on them.
- **`flash_centre` not removed from the return tuple** (§7.5).
- **Nothing committed.** The working tree holds the change; no commit was made,
  as none was requested.

## 9. What Only the Target Can Answer

1. **Is 7 px enough for the boundary marks** now that they carry the whole
   anticipatory cue, against 7 px major ticks distinguished only by colour?
   Single literal at `manager.py:1315`.
2. **Is the day-band-3 centre at luminance 0.0927 comfortable at night**, or in
   a dark tunnel in day mode? It is the brightest of the twelve and sits just
   inside the ceiling.
3. **Does the uniform sweep actually reduce the read time** relative to the
   graduated one, in peripheral vision while driving? That is the entire
   premise of `issue-64d8d8fc` and the bench cannot evaluate it.
4. **Is the idle→torque-approach transition legible** given bands 0 and 1 share
   a blue and the sweep is now uniform (§7.2)?
5. **Does the band-coloured flashing centre read as an upshift imperative**, or
   does removing the green from the centre weaken it? The border is still green
   and the contrast is better in night (§7.3), but the semantic shift from
   "green means shift" to "band colour flashing means shift" is a human
   judgement.
